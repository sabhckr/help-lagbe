Allegations are private by default

A report submitted to this platform is not a publication. It is a private case, read by trained workers. Submitting a report does not make anyone guilty, and we do not publish allegations because they were made. Most cases are never published at all; they are resolved through information, referral and follow-up.

The path to publication

A story is published only after every one of these steps:

  1. Consent and safety review. The people whose experiences appear in the story agree to publication, and a safeguarding review confirms that publishing will not put them or others at greater risk.
  2. Source and evidence assessment. Editors assess what the evidence shows and what it does not, and record which claims are supported.
  3. Redaction. Names, addresses, phone numbers, identity documents, intimate imagery, sensitive evidence and anything that could identify a child are removed.
  4. Editorial review by a second person. Someone who did not write the piece checks it against the evidence and against this policy.
  5. Legal review where needed. Pieces that name, or could identify, a person or institution are reviewed by a lawyer.
  6. Approval. A responsible editor approves publication and records the decision.

Labels

Every published piece carries one of these labels so that readers know what kind of claim they are reading:

  • Reported allegation: a claim made to us, not independently established.
  • Corroborated finding: a claim supported by evidence we have examined ourselves.
  • Official statement: what an institution or authority has said, presented as their statement.
  • Court-established outcome: a result recorded by a court or formal tribunal.
  • Awareness material: general information that does not describe a specific case.

Right of response

Before we publish an allegation about an identifiable person or institution, we tell them what we intend to publish and give them an opportunity to respond. Their response, or the fact that they did not respond, is reported. The right of response does not apply where it would endanger a source or a child, and that decision is recorded with reasons.

What we never publish

Children's identifying details, private addresses, phone numbers, identity documents, intimate imagery and sensitive evidence are never published, whatever the public interest. We do not publish a person's name as an accused party on the strength of a report alone.

Corrections and requests

When we get something wrong, we correct it visibly on the piece and list the correction on our corrections page. Anyone can request a correction, a reply, an appeal or a removal. Withdrawn pieces are taken down and the withdrawal is recorded.

Separation from casework

Editorial staff do not decide the outcome of cases, and case workers do not decide what is published. A worker who handled a case can be a source of information for a story but does not approve it.
