What safeguarding means here

Safeguarding is the set of rules that govern how this platform protects the people who use it, especially children and anyone at risk of harm. These rules apply to every worker, every case and every conversation.

Immediate danger

This platform does not dispatch police, ambulances, fire services or rescue. If someone is in immediate danger, contact the emergency numbers in the verified contacts box on this page, if you can do so safely. We will tell you this clearly whenever a case suggests immediate risk, and we will not pretend to be a service we are not.

How risk is assessed

Every report is read by a trained person, who assigns one of four severity levels and writes down the reasoning. The levels describe urgency and potential harm. They are not a judgment of anyone's guilt, and they are not a ranking of how believable a report is.

  • Level 1: routine information or a non-urgent concern.
  • Level 2: an elevated concern that needs timely review.
  • Level 3: high concern, such as credible threats, stalking, coercive blackmail or ongoing abuse.
  • Level 4: critical, immediate danger or an urgent child-safety concern.

Lack of evidence never means low risk. Many people in danger have no documents, no screenshots and no witnesses. The level is reviewed as a case develops, and it can go up or down, with a written reason each time.

Children and young people

A concern about a child is treated as urgent. Identifying details of a child are never published, never shared outside the case team without a safeguarding reason, and never used in awareness material. If a report suggests a child is at immediate risk, the worker's duty to protect the child may require them to disclose information to an appropriate authority, even against the wishes of the person who reported. We say this openly rather than promising a secrecy we cannot keep.

Who sees your information

Case narratives are visible to the workers assigned to the case and the specialists they call on. Identity details in a confidential report are stored separately, encrypted, and can only be opened by an assigned worker who records a written reason; every access is logged and reviewed. Access can be restricted further for safeguarding material or legally privileged material.

Limits of confidentiality

We protect your information carefully, but we cannot promise that nothing will ever be disclosed. A court order, a serious risk to someone's life, or a duty to protect a child could require disclosure. If this ever applies to your case, the decision is made by a person with written reasoning, and limited to what is necessary.

What we never do

  • We never contact a family member, employer or alleged perpetrator unless you explicitly request it.
  • We never advise anyone to confront an abuser.
  • We never call anyone guilty because a report was submitted.
  • We never use automated systems or AI to talk to people seeking support.

Staff conduct

Workers are trained in safeguarding and in the limits of their role. They may not seek your identity without a recorded reason, may not contact you outside the platform's channels unless you have asked for that, and may not work on a case they have a personal connection to. If you have a concern about how a worker treated you, use the complaints process; a complaint about a worker is never handled by that worker.
