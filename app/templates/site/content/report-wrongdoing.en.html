Before you begin

This platform does not dispatch police, ambulances or rescue. If someone is in immediate danger, use the emergency numbers in the verified contacts box on this page, if you can do so safely. Reporting here is for situations where it would help to have a trained person read your account, give you information, and help you reach the right service.

What you can report

You can report harm to yourself or to another person: bullying, harassment on the street or online, sexual harassment, abuse of a child, violence at home, blackmail, threats, exploitation, misuse of your online accounts or images, and bribery or corruption by people in positions of power. If your situation does not fit a category, choose "other" and describe it in your own words. You can also ask a legal question. You do not need to be the person it happened to.

How to write a useful report

  • Say what happened, when, where and who was involved, as far as you know. Approximate dates are fine.
  • If you have evidence such as screenshots, messages, photographs or documents, you can attach it. Keep the originals. Make sure the date and the account name are visible in each screenshot.
  • Do not put yourself at risk to gather more evidence. A report without evidence is still a valid report, and lack of evidence never means we treat a situation as low risk.
  • Tell us if you are in danger now or might be soon. This affects how quickly a person looks at your case.

Anonymous or confidential

You choose when you submit. An anonymous report collects no identity at all; staff can only reach you through Track My Case, which you open with the reference and one-time key shown once after submitting. A confidential report stores your contact details separately from your account of events, encrypted, and only an assigned worker who records a written reason can open them; every access is logged and reviewed. Neither option is absolute secrecy. A court order, a serious risk to life, or a child-protection duty could require disclosure, and we will not promise otherwise.

Severity levels

A trained person reads every report and assigns a level with written reasoning. The levels describe urgency and potential harm, never guilt.

  • Level 1: routine information or a non-urgent concern.
  • Level 2: an elevated concern that needs timely review.
  • Level 3: high concern, such as credible threats, stalking, coercive blackmail or ongoing abuse.
  • Level 4: critical, immediate danger or an urgent child-safety concern.

What happens next

Your case moves through clear stages: submitted, triage, assigned, under review, specialist review, referral or action, follow-up, and resolved or closed. You can appeal a decision, and a closed case can be reopened if something changes. Outcomes are recorded truthfully: referral offered, complaint submitted with confirmation, platform action confirmed, official outcome verified, investigation ongoing, or outcome unknown.

What we will not do

We never contact a family member, employer or the person you have reported unless you explicitly ask. We do not advise anyone to confront an abuser. Submitting a report does not make anyone guilty, and we do not publish allegations; a story can only be published after the process described in our editorial policy, and only with consent.
